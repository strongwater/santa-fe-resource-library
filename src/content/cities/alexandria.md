<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What happened in Alexandria</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card clay">
<p>"Zoning for Housing" ended single-family-only zoning in 2023–24; a legal challenge filed in January 2024 was dismissed in November 2025, upholding the reform.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card clay">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Original density-cap policy (pre-2023)</b>"Areas of the city currently zoned residential should remain zoned for residential use at no higher than their current density."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Amended text, MPA #2023-00005</b>Same clause with the density cap struck: "Areas of the City currently zoned residential should remain zoned for residential use" — the cap itself removed from the Master Plan, not just the zoning code.</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Equity mandate added</b>"Ensure race and social equity is 'incorporated and centered in all planning'... including... all references to preserving and protecting neighborhoods and character."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Measurable target</b>"The City shall uphold the commitment contained in Resolution 830 to maintain 1150 publicly-assisted housing units."</p>
</div>
<p>"Zoning for Housing" was formally grounded in an amended Master Plan (a struck density-cap clause, a new equity mandate) enacted alongside the zoning text amendment — giving the reform an explicit, adopted policy record for courts to point to as legislative intent. A judge dismissed the legal challenge in Nov. 2025.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> when removing a density cap or upzoning, amend the actual General Plan text — not just zoning code — and pair the change with an explicit, citable finding or policy statement it implements.</p>
