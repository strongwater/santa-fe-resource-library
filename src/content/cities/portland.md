<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Portland did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Residential Infill Project legalized duplex through fourplex (six units if income-restricted), ADUs, and cottage clusters across zones covering 75% of the city's residential land. RIP2 added rowhouses and larger-lot flexibility.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>1,400+</b> units permitted, Aug 2021–Jun 2024</span>
<span class="stat"><b>43%</b> of citywide permits from single-dwelling zones (up from 10–20%)</span>
<span class="stat"><b>$200–300k</b> cheaper than new detached homes</span>
</div>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"Portland is a prosperous, healthy, equitable and resilient city where everyone has access to opportunity and is engaged in shaping decisions that affect their lives."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 3.2 — Growth and stability</b>"Direct the majority of growth and change to centers, corridors, and transit station areas, allowing the continuation of the scale and characteristics of Portland's residential neighborhoods."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 3.18</b>"Design centers to be compact, safe, attractive, and accessible places, where the street environment makes access by transit, walking, biking, and mobility devices... safe and attractive for people of all ages and abilities."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Chapter 3 narrative</b>"This network of well-functioning centers and corridors will be critical to achieve the Portland Plan goals to make it possible for most Portlanders (80 percent) to live in complete neighborhoods by 2035."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 3.36</b>Calls for housing capacity "to accommodate 3,500 households" within a half-mile walk of designated Neighborhood Centers.</p>
</div>
<p>This is the umbrella plan the Residential Infill Project implements. It names <em>where</em> growth goes and explicitly trades that off against protecting existing neighborhood scale elsewhere, backed by numeric capacity targets ("80% in complete neighborhoods," "3,500 households").</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> pair every centers/corridors goal with a numeric capacity or mode-share target; state the growth-direction trade-off explicitly rather than leaving it implicit.</p>
