<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Minneapolis did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>First major U.S. city to end single-family-only zoning citywide. Paired duplex/triplex legalization with phased parking-minimum elimination (2009–2021) and transit-corridor upzoning (3–6 stories on corridors, 10–30 near light rail).</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>~21,000</b> units permitted, 2017–22</span>
<span class="stat"><b>+12%</b> housing stock vs. 4% statewide</span>
<span class="stat"><b>1% rent growth</b> vs. 14% statewide</span>
</div>
<p class="muted">Caveat: 87% of new units were in 20+ unit buildings — the duplex/triplex legalization alone drove only ~1% of permits. The parking and corridor moves did most of the production work.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">The 4(d) Affordable Housing Incentive</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Beyond the 2040 Plan's zoning changes above, Minneapolis runs a property-tax incentive: landlords who keep roughly a fifth of their units affordable and cap rent increases get a reduced property-tax classification for a ten-year term.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>2,338</b> units enrolled in 2024 alone — 5x the historical average</span>
</div>
<p class="muted">Tightened in November 2024 after the city found many enrolled units were already renting near the qualifying price.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> a property-tax carrot reaches buildings that already exist, not just new construction — a useful complement to a zoning change that only affects what gets built next.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"Done right, growth can help our city become a healthy, sustainable, and thriving place for all."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Goal 9 — Complete Neighborhoods</b>"In 2040, all Minneapolis residents will have access to employment, retail services, healthy food, parks, and other daily needs via walking, biking, and public transit."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 6, action step</b>"Eliminate the requirement for off-street parking minimums throughout the city."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 58 — Business Districts and Corridors</b>"Support thriving business districts and corridors that build on cultural assets and serve the needs of Minneapolis residents."</p>
</div>
<p>~100 numbered policies, each a one-sentence directive plus numbered action steps naming real regulatory levers. Twelve future land-use categories map one-to-one onto fourteen "Built Form Districts" carrying actual height/FAR/setback standards.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> the goal format "In [year], [outcome] via [named mechanisms]"; numbering every policy for trackability; a visible policy-to-zoning-district crosswalk.</p>
