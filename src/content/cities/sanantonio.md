<section class="sub" style="padding-top:8px;">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE APPROACH</div><h2 class="section-title">San Antonio's tiered system</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Full historic districts and individual landmarks get the strongest tax incentives and design review. Neighborhood Conservation Districts (NCDs) — a lighter-touch overlay first used in Alta Vista (2003) — protect neighborhood scale and character (setbacks, lot coverage, some design elements) without the full review burden of historic-district status, a middle option between no protection and full designation.</p>
<p>On the incentive side, a "10-Year Tax Freeze" waives city property tax for 5 years after a qualifying rehabilitation (at least 30% of the improvement's value), then taxes only 50% of the post-rehab value for years 6–10 — automatic for qualifying landmark and district properties, not a competitive grant. New historic-district homeowners separately get a flat 20% city tax exemption for 10 years (15 if they stay the full term).</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>469</b> properties have used the rehabilitation incentive since 2002, across 13 zip codes citywide</span>
<span class="stat">average participant saves <b>~$4,800</b> in city property taxes</span>
</div>
<p class="muted">Some owners describe the incentive application process as time-consuming enough to be a participation barrier, by the city's own reporting. Alta Vista's NCD needed a 2018 ordinance amendment — 15 years after its 2003 creation — because the original guidelines had become outdated, a reminder that a conservation-district tool needs periodic recalibration, not a one-time adoption.</p>
