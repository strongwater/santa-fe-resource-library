<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Spokane did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>The best-documented model for middle-housing dimensional standards: no max density under 2 acres, 1,200 sq ft minimum lot, 65–80% lot coverage, 40-ft height limit, generous alley ADUs, parking waived within a half-mile of transit. CNU and Strong Towns hold it up as the national standard for simple, objective code language over discretionary review.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>455+</b> units permitted under interim rules alone</span>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">Multifamily Tax Exemption</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Beyond the middle-housing dimensional standards above, Spokane also runs Washington's statewide Multifamily Tax Exemption program, layering a property-tax carrot on top of its own zoning reform.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Land use vision</b>"Growth will be managed to allow a mix of land uses that fit, support, and enhance Spokane's neighborhoods, protect the environment, and sustain the downtown area..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LU 1.4</b>"Direct new higher density residential uses to Centers and Corridors designated on the Land Use Plan Map."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Table LU-1</b>Sets numeric mandatory use-mix targets per center tier — Neighborhood Centers: 10% public / 20% commercial / 40% higher-density housing.</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LU 3.6 (compact/missing-middle)</b>Defines "compact housing" by name — "townhouses, accessory dwelling units (granny flats), live-work housing, triplexes, zero-lot line, starter, small-lot, and row houses" — allowed "in all neighborhoods."</p>
</div>
<p>The tightest plan-to-code linkage in this set: Policy LU 3.6 names the exact housing typologies the 2023 middle-housing ordinance then legalized citywide, and Table LU-1 sets numeric density/use-mix targets rather than vague "encourage a mix" language. One vision sentence → one goal → numbered policies with quantified thresholds and named housing forms, almost one-to-one onto zoning standards.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> pair each vision statement with 1-2 goals, then policies naming specific building/lot typologies plus at least one quantitative table, so the zoning code has an unambiguous policy citation to implement.</p>
