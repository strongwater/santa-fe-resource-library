<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Sacramento did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Missing Middle Housing ordinance paired with the 2040 General Plan — called by Opticos Design "one of the most progressive missing middle strategies in the U.S." As of August 2026, local reporting finds construction still lagging well behind legalization.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"In 2040, the City of Sacramento will be a national model of sustainable, equitable growth and community development."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Goal LUP-4</b>"Walkable, transit-oriented centers and corridors that concentrate new jobs, housing, and entertainment opportunities to support frequent, reliable transit service."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LUP-4.1</b>"The City shall encourage increased residential and commercial development intensity within ½-mile of existing high-frequency bus stops and existing and planned light rail... stations."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LUP-6.3 — Missing Middle</b>Names the actual typologies: "duplexes, triplexes, fourplexes, cottage/bungalow courts, neighborhood-scale multi-unit buildings" sitting "in the middle of a spectrum between detached single-unit homes and mid-rise apartment buildings."</p>
</div>
<div class="note"><b>Honest note:</b> policies attach real numeric triggers (½-mile radius, named housing types) but the plan sets intensity/location targets without guaranteeing approval speed, financing, or infrastructure funding — consistent with local reporting that construction is lagging well behind the legalization.</div>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> name exact housing types and a geographic trigger (distance-to-transit) in policy text — but pair that language with the implementation mechanisms Sacramento's plan itself lacks.</p>
