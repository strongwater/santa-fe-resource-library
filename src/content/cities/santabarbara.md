<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Santa Barbara did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>The closest comparable among historic, tourism-economy coastal cities with strict design-review traditions. Uses a "suitable sites inventory" and unit-lot subdivision tools to fit multiple smaller units on sites that still read as one historic-scaled building — directly relevant to Santa Fe's historic-district overlay concerns.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">The Average Unit-Size Density (AUD) Program</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Santa Barbara's own AUD Incentive Program (2013) allows up to 63 units/acre in exchange for keeping units near 1,000 sq ft — letting a historic-scaled building hold more homes without changing its footprint, on top of the statewide density bonus and ADU framework every California city builds under.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>El Pueblo Viejo historic district</b>"The purpose of El Pueblo Viejo is to preserve and enhance the unique heritage and architectural character of the central area of the city... while allowing reasonable development."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Average Unit-Size Density Incentive Program</b>"Encourage smaller, more affordable units through established unit sizes, while allowing flexibility for larger units, which help subsidize the cost of the smaller units." Target unit size: "approximately 1,000 square feet, sufficient to accommodate two bedrooms."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Housing Element, Program HE-6</b>"The City shall review objective design standards to ensure they do not unnecessarily constrain housing production."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>AUD Program (2013)</b>Allows "increased densities (up to 63 units/acre)" in exchange for the unit-size formula above.</p>
</div>
<p>The closest comparable among historic, tourism-economy, strict-design-review cities. Historic preservation is explicitly bounded ("while allowing reasonable development"), not an unqualified trump card.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> adopt one concrete numeric mechanism (a unit-size-based density formula, or similar by-right envelope rule) that operationalizes the historic-character/housing tradeoff inside the existing height/FAR envelope, plus a standing commitment to audit whether design standards constrain housing production.</p>
