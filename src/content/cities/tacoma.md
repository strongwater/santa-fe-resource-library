<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">Multifamily Tax Exemption</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Beyond Home in Tacoma's zoning changes above, Tacoma is one of more than 50 Washington cities using the state's Multifamily Tax Exemption — an 8/12/20-year property-tax exemption on a new multifamily building's improvement value, with longer terms tied to deeper affordability.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>550</b> units across 15 projects in 2025 — the most projects of any Washington city that year</span>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"Every Tacoma resident is a safe and short walk, roll, bus, train, or bike ride away from daily essentials and community amenities, such as groceries, schools, parks, and healthcare."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Goal GS-5</b>"Tacoma's growth is focused in a citywide network of transit-connected centers that anchor 15-minute neighborhoods providing nodes of activity and access to housing, employment, and services."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Density standard, Exhibit 18</b>"Regional Growth Centers are expected to plan for land use that accommodates at least 45 activity units (population and jobs) per gross acre."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy CN-2.7</b>"Introduce a system of compact blocks (300-400 feet in block face length) where opportunities to do so are feasible."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy H-1.2 / Section 3.1</b>"Middle housing types, such as townhomes, 2-3-4-plexes, and cottage housing are allowed across most all residential districts in Tacoma," against a stated need of "42,865 units... by 2044."</p>
</div>
<p>A tiered center hierarchy where each tier carries a hard numeric intensity target, not just a label; "walkability" becomes a testable standard (15-minute access, 300–400 ft block faces); housing policy is pinned to an actual production number.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> a mapped center hierarchy with a distinct numeric target per tier; a measurable "complete neighborhood" walk-time test; citywide default legalization of middle-housing types tied to a production number.</p>
