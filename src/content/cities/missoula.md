<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Missoula did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Directly comparable: university town, mountain-valley setting, tourism economy, acute housing-cost crisis. "Our Missoula" is now driving a full zoning-code rewrite; an October 2025 draft would allow apartments in most of the city. The process has deliberately emphasized resident involvement — worth studying the engagement model specifically.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision/values</b>"We believe that in order to achieve a healthy community and environment we must increase housing availability and access at all levels in all neighborhoods, enhance resiliency and adaptability, emphasize connectivity..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Centers/corridors</b>"Unlock the growth potential for mixed-use centers and corridors in order to allow more people to live within walking distance of their daily needs."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Housing distribution</b>Calls on all neighborhoods to "take on their fair share" of the city's housing needs.</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Concrete mechanism</b>Replaces Euclidean zoning with 12 "place types" and 8 "street types"; every residential place type permits a minimum of two units per parcel; a stated need of 22,000–27,500 new units by 2045; over 150 discrete implementation actions tied to code amendments.</p>
</div>
<div class="note"><b>Honest note:</b> the vision/values chapter itself reads fairly generic — the specificity lives in the land-use strategy and implementation chapters, not the opening vision. A plan can afford a warmer, less clinical vision statement as long as what follows is this concrete. Missoula followed through with a new Unified Development Code within about a year.</div>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow (near-identical peer city):</b> a place-type/street-type system instead of a vague future-land-use map; a numbered implementation-action table tied to code sections; a minimum-density floor stated as an enforceable number.</p>
