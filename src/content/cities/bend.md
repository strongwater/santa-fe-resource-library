<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Bend did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Fast-growing high-desert resort city implementing Oregon's statewide Climate-Friendly and Equitable Communities rules — designated walkable, mixed-use "Climate Friendly Areas" with reduced parking mandates, on top of state-required middle-housing code. A model for concentrating walkable-center intensity rather than upzoning uniformly.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Goal, Ch. 11</b>"Create new walkable, mixed use and complete communities by leveraging and complementing land use patterns inside the existing boundary..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 11-32</b>"The City will support re-designation of suitable areas that are within a ¼ mile walk to transit corridors from a lower density designation to a higher density designation."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 11-51 / Table 11-1</b>Master plans require minimum density standards from <b>2.0 dwellings/gross acre</b> (Urban Low Density) up to <b>21.7 dwellings/gross acre</b> (Urban High Density), plus a required housing-type mix.</p>
</div>
<p>The vision statement is soft civic-pride language, but Chapter 11 becomes concrete fast: named geographies (Central Core, Opportunity Areas, transit corridors), a numeric density floor per designation, and a bright-line spatial test (¼-mile walk to transit) rather than "encourage density near transit."</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> keep an aspirational vision paragraph, but follow it immediately with a typology table — Urban Center / Corridor / Neighborhood Center — each carrying a minimum density number and a walk-distance trigger for upzoning.</p>
