<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Berkeley did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Symbolically significant: the city that invented single-family zoning in 1916 voted to end it. A follow-on package moving through 2024–25 would allow taller, denser housing citywide beyond the initial repeal.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">California's density bonus &amp; SB 35</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Berkeley builds under California's statewide density bonus and ADU framework — and was the site of the first appellate test of SB 35's streamlining requirement.</p>
<p class="muted"><em>Ruegg &amp; Ellsworth v. City of Berkeley</em> (2021) forced the city to ministerially approve a 260-unit mixed-use project it had tried to block on historic-preservation grounds — establishing that SB 35's by-right approval applies even over local historic-designation objections.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision framing</b>"Berkeley represents an intricate and delicate balance that is constantly undergoing subtle changes in its physical and social fabric."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Goal 1</b>"Preserve Berkeley's unique character and quality of life."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LU-26 / LU-27</b>Names actual districts rather than generic categories: "Maintain and improve Neighborhood Commercial areas, such as Elmwood, Solano, and North Shattuck, as pedestrian-friendly, visually attractive areas..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LU-23</b>"Encourage and maintain zoning that allows greater commercial and residential density and reduced residential parking requirements in areas with above-average transit service such as Downtown Berkeley."</p>
</div>
<div class="note"><b>Honest note:</b> this reads as typical early-2000s comp-plan language — soft, "character and balance" framing at the vision level. Notably, "missing middle" and the 2023 single-family-zoning repeal appear nowhere in the General Plan text itself: Berkeley made that change as a standalone zoning ordinance rather than a General Plan amendment, so there's a clear opportunity to bring the plan's own housing-type language up to date.</div>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> naming specific corridors/districts in policy text makes it enforceable — and updating the plan alongside a major zoning reform keeps that reform backed by the comprehensive plan, not just the zoning code.</p>
