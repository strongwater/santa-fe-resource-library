<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Somerville did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Not a physical or economic peer, but the most-cited "state of the art code for a walkable city" (CNU's phrase). Ties allowed density directly to walkability and transit-access metrics rather than blanket citywide rules — a useful structural model if Santa Fe wants to concentrate intensity around specific corridors and nodes.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">AFFORDABLE HOUSING INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">Massachusetts' Chapter 40B backdrop</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>SomerVision didn't create Chapter 40B, but every Massachusetts city's housing production sits against it — a comprehensive-permit override active statewide since 1969, letting developers bypass local zoning in towns below 10% affordable housing in exchange for reserving 20–25% of units as long-term affordable.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>~70,000</b> homes produced statewide since 1969</span>
<span class="stat"><b>35,000+</b> of those income-restricted</span>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"SomerVision is an easy-to-use guide for future growth and development in the City, providing a policy framework for other planning efforts including neighborhood plans and capital plans."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Headline targets (public dashboard)</b>"30,000 new jobs" · "6,000 new housing units – 1,200 permanently affordable" · "125 new acres of publicly-accessible open space" · "85% of new development in transformative areas."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Goal VI</b>"Transform key opportunity areas, such as Assembly Square, Inner Belt, Brickbottom, Boynton Yards and the southeastern portion of Union Square into dynamic, mixed-use and transit-oriented districts..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Fiscal lever</b>Tiered inclusionary zoning (20% affordable for 18+ unit projects) plus a housing linkage fee of "$10 per square foot" on large commercial development.</p>
</div>
<p>Nearly every goal pairs a countable target with a named geography — not "encourage transit-oriented development" but "85% of new development in transformative areas," where the areas are named on a map. Progress is tracked on a public dashboard.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> pick a handful of numeric, trackable 20-year targets; name the actual places growth is meant to go; attach a real fiscal or zoning lever to each target.</p>
