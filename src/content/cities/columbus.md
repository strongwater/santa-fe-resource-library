<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"Columbus will embrace and guide growth to build a future that provides opportunities for all residents."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Density standard, Mixed Use 1</b>"Guideline: 24 units/acre OR 72 bedrooms/acre — Heights of 3–4 stories."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Density standard, Mixed Use 3</b>"Guideline: Unlimited units/acre — Heights of 8+ stories."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Guiding Principle 1a</b>"Mixed-use and highest density residential development should be focused along primary corridors, at established commercial centers, and at locations recommended for mixed-use development with access to... transit."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Housing variety</b>Names "missing middle housing" explicitly alongside "accessory dwelling units (ADUs), age-friendly housing, and universally designed housing" as a stated priority for all neighborhoods.</p>
</div>
<p>The vision line is generic, but it's immediately anchored to concrete, numeric standards — units/acre <em>and</em> bedrooms/acre thresholds paired with story-height ranges per tier — exactly the testable language a generic "encourage appropriate density" policy lacks.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> pair each land-use category with an explicit density-and-height table; state missing-middle/ADU support as a named priority rather than folding it into generic "housing diversity" language.</p>
