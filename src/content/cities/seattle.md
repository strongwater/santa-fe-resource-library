<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE INCENTIVE</div><h2 class="section-title">How Seattle's MFTE works</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Under the state's Multifamily Tax Exemption framework (RCW 84.14), Seattle exempts the residential improvement value of new or rehabbed multifamily buildings from property tax for 8, 12, or 20 years — longer terms require a deeper share of income-restricted units. The program applies only within designated walkable urban centers and villages, not citywide.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat"><b>303 projects</b>, 33,956 total units since 1998</span>
<span class="stat"><b>7,047</b> permanently income-restricted units (6,636 active today)</span>
<span class="stat"><b>1,950 units</b>, 10 projects in 2025 alone — the top-producing city in the state</span>
</div>
<p class="muted">A University of Washington evaluation found benefit-to-cost ratios of only 46–78% depending on program vintage, and roughly $35–80 million a year in foregone property tax shifted to other taxpayers — the city's own October 2025 reforms (tighter income/rent limits, a $10,000-to-$300-per-unit fee increase) responded directly to that audit, sunsetting the current program design in September 2029.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">HISTORIC PRESERVATION</div><h2 class="section-title">Citizen-led designation, public ownership</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>The Pike Place Market Historical District was created by a 1971 citizen ballot initiative, after voters overturned a 1963 urban-renewal plan that would have demolished most of the market. A "Friends of the Market" campaign, led by architect Victor Steinbrueck, put the district's protection directly to a public vote rather than leaving it to a council decision. A 12-member citizen Historical Commission now reviews changes within the district, and the Pike Place Market Preservation and Development Authority (PDA), created in 1973, owns roughly 80% of the properties in the district — a public-development-authority ownership model rather than regulation of many private owners, giving the preservation body direct control over maintenance and tenanting, not only design-review veto power.</p>
<div class="stat-row">
<span class="stat">~<b>500</b> residents, ~90% low-income seniors, across 4 named buildings</span>
<span class="stat"><b>20.9M</b> visitors and <b>$177M</b> in commercial sales, 2023</span>
</div>
<p class="muted">The market remains a full working district rather than a museum piece. Research for this library did not surface a well-documented account of a major trade-off or controversy here comparable to this library's other preservation case studies — worth noting honestly rather than implying one exists; the PDA's ownership model and citizen-commission review may simply generate less public friction, or that friction may be thinly documented in secondary sources.</p>
