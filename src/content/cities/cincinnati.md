<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>Ties zoning reform directly to transit: reduced parking minimums and increased density along transit lines and commercial corridors. The multi-hearing rollout offers real, instructive lessons for engagement design.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"An unapologetic drive to create and sustain a thriving inclusive urban community, where engaged people and memorable places are paramount... where local pride and confidence are contagious."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Neighborhood centers concept</b>"Our neighborhoods are structured around centers of activity that contain all of the amenities that we need to go about our daily life. We will focus our development on these centers of activity..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>"Live" initiative strategy</b>"Become more walkable" and "Provide a full spectrum of housing options, and improve housing quality and affordability."</p>
</div>
<div class="note"><b>Honest note:</b> vivid and specific about physical form (walkable, mixed-use centers), with room to strengthen the goal/strategy layer ("Become more walkable," "Strengthen our magnetic city") with numeric targets, timelines, or "shall" language. Pairing that vision with specific, agreed-upon zoning detail up front — before a Connected-Communities-style translation into by-right density arrives — is what earns lasting public buy-in.</div>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> the strong sensory/form-based vision language is worth emulating, but pair it with genuinely measurable, geographically-specific policies in the plan itself so zoning implementation isn't the first time specifics appear.</p>
