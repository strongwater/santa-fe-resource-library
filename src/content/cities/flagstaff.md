<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Flagstaff did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p><strong>Likely the closest national peer to Santa Fe</strong> — similar population, historic downtown, university presence, high-desert setting, severe housing-cost burden, heavy tourism. Arizona's HB 2721 forced Flagstaff to legalize duplex–fourplex housing near its core; the resulting citywide code drew broad public support at hearings, unlike comparable proposals elsewhere. Flagstaff's code was already form-based, a useful precedent for historic-character concerns.</p>

<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Policy LU.2.1 (2030)</b>"Design new neighborhoods that embody the characteristics of Flagstaff's favorite neighborhoods – that is, with a mix of uses, a variety of housing types and densities."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Regional Plan 2045, Policy DP.2</b>"Support development and redevelopment with a diversity of housing types by expanding opportunities for missing middle housing."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Regional Plan 2045, Policy WR.4</b>"Provide sustainable and reliable water and sewer services within the urban growth boundary" — paired with: "Do not extend water and sewer services beyond the urban growth boundary unless an analysis shows the service can be provided without impacting the availability of water to property owners already within the boundary."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Zoning Code §10-00.060</b>Six named transect zones from T1 (rural preserve) to T6 (downtown core), with T5 defined as main-street commercial corridors — "character area" language translated directly into a mapped, buildable zoning ordinance.</p>
</div>
<p><strong>Likely the single closest national peer to Santa Fe</strong> — similar population, historic downtown, university presence, high-desert setting, severe housing-cost burden, heavy tourism. Its 2045 plan was approved by voters in May 2026, an unusually fresh comparable.</p>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> write every land-use goal as a short, numbered policy a future zoning amendment can cite by number; pair the plan with a transect/character-area zoning framework so "compact, walkable, historic-compatible" becomes mapped, enforceable rules.</p>
