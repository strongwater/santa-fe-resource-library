<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">THE ZONING REFORM</div><h2 class="section-title">What Boulder did</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<p>"Zoning for Affordable Housing" and expanding ADU rules now layer on top of Colorado's 2024 statewide land-use package, which preempted some local restrictions — a model for how state pressure can do political work local council would otherwise absorb alone.</p>

<section class="sub">
<div class="section-head"><div class="section-num">GENERAL PLAN LANGUAGE</div><h2 class="section-title">In the plan's own words</h2></div>
<div class="card-grid">
<article class="card">
<div class="quote-stack">
<p class="quote"><b>Vision</b>"The Boulder Valley community honors its history and legacy of planning for a livable community surrounded by open space and rural lands while striving together to create and preserve a truly special place that is sustainable, resilient, equitable and inclusive..."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 2.03</b>Development should "take advantage of existing urban services, and avoid, insofar as possible, patterns of leapfrog, noncontiguous, scattered development."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Policy 2.19, Neighborhood Centers</b>"Neighborhood centers contribute to a sense of place and the achievement of walkable (15-minute) places with a mix of uses and range of services."</p>
<p class="quote"><b>Historic commitment</b>"An inclusionary zoning ordinance was adopted, requiring 20 percent of new residential development in the city to be permanently affordable."</p>
</div>
<div class="note"><b>Honest note:</b> Boulder's centers/density language leans more "will encourage... in appropriate locations" than a hard mandate — reflecting a growth-boundary tradition focused more on limiting growth than channeling it into dense, walkable form. A useful example of why pairing "encourage" language with firmer standards strengthens even a well-organized, numbered plan. A full new BVCP update was just adopted Aug 21, 2026; its text isn't public yet.</div>
<p class="borrow"><b>Borrow:</b> the numbered-policy-tied-to-a-map format, the specific 20% inclusionary number, and the "15-minute" metric — while writing Santa Fe's center/density policies as clearer mandates than Boulder's "encourage" language.</p>
